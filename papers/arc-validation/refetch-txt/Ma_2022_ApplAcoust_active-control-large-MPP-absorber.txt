a b s t r a c t
This paper presents a theoretical investigation on actively controlling the low frequency sound absorption of large-sized micro-perforated panel absorber (MPPA) by using point source placed in the cavity.
The large sized active MPPA is an easily implemented and cost-effective scheme for achieving superior
low frequency sound absorption in large area. The modal analysis approach is used to establish the theoretical model of such active MPPA. The physical mechanism of active control is analyzed and some physical insights are summarized. Based on these conclusions, the error sensing strategy of the large sized
active MPPA is also constructed. Finally, experimental tests are carried out to validate the theoretical
modeling and findings. Results show that the sound absorption performance can be significantly
improved after control in a wide low frequency range below the cutoff frequency. The optimal position
of the point source is at the central of the cavity section, in which case the cutoff frequency is the largest
and depends on the length of the short side of the active MPPA. The control mechanism of sound absorption improvement is to enhance the Helmholtz type resonant absorption by suppressing the cavity sound
field. The high order cavity mode (except for (0,0,0) mode) has no contribution to sound absorption
improvement due to the symmetrical property of their mode shape. The pressure release (PR) and impedance matching (IM) strategies are still applicable for the large sized active MPPA. Relative small sized
cavity or low frequency excitation can guarantee the uniformity of the cavity sound field after control,
which is conducive to achieve remarkable improvement of sound absorption.

1. Introduction
Due to enough acoustic resistance and low acoustic mass reactance, micro-perforated panel (MPP) has highly efficient sound
absorption performance and has been widely used as a classical
type of sound absorption materials. The excellent properties of
light, fiberless, and cleanliness also allow MPP to be used in especial environments where severe hygienic conditions are imposed,
like in hospital or food industries. In practice, the most commonly
used form is the micro-perforated panel absorber (MPPA) in which
the MPP is located in front of a rigid backing wall [1]. The shallow
air cavity in the MPPA can generate resonance, which increases the
particle velocity through the holes and enhances the dissipation of
sound energy.

also developed for broadening low frequency absorption bandwidth. The passive methods mainly include MPPA backed by Helmholtz resonators [20], or MPPA backed with mechanical impedance
plates [21]. These methods introduce resonant structure to obtain
the improvement of sound absorption in a very narrow bandwidth.
Tao et.al [22] proposed the MPPA with a circuit shunted loudspeaker as the backing wall, which adjusts the acoustic impedance
of the loudspeaker diaphragm to improve the low frequency sound
absorption in a relative wide range. On the contrary, active control
technic can achieve sound absorption improvement in a wide low
frequency range. For one-dimensional duct with plane-wave incident excitation, an active control system which used a piston
source in place of the backing wall is proposed to actively control
the sound absorption of the MPPA [23–25]. The impedance matching (IM) and pressure release (PR) strategies are also developed.
The IM strategy adjusts the velocity of the piston to maximally
absorb the reflected plane wave in the cavity. The PR strategy
adjusts the velocity of the piston so that the incident plane wave
can be cancelled by the reflected wave in the cavity. These two
strategies essentially weaken the acoustic reactance of the cavity,
which indirectly adjusts the surface impedance of the MPPA to
match that of air.
The lumped parameter loudspeaker is used as the piston source
for the above research [25,26], in which case the active MPPA is
merely a small sized sound absorber element. There needs a lot
of elements to construct the large area sound absorption layer
(seen as in Ref. [24]), which will become difficult to realize in engineering. On the contrary, when the control element is constructed
by a relative large sized MPPA with a loudspeaker placed in the
cavity close to the backing wall, there will need small number of
the elements to construct the large area sound absorption layer.
The system will become easier to implement. The surface impedance of the large sized active MPPA can also be adjusted to match
the characteristic impedance of air by actively controlling the cavity sound field. However, the loudspeaker is more similar to the
point source than the piston source in the large sized case. The control performance and mechanism will be different from that of the
small sized case. The effectiveness of the IM and PR strategies also
remains to be verified. Hence, the purpose of this research is to fill
in the above gaps, which are of great significance for engineering
application of such technic.
Since the modal analysis approach can clearly reflect the vibroacoustic coupling characteristics of the MPPA and the physical nature of sound absorption [27,28], it is also applied to establish the
theoretical model of the proposed large sized active MPPA. Then,
the control mechanism is analyzed by comparing the cavity sound
field in controlled and uncontrolled conditions. According to these
findings, the error sensing strategy is also constructed based on the
PR and IM strategies.
The remainder of this paper is organized as follows. The theoretical model is established in Section 2. A detailed investigation
on control mechanism is presented in Section 3. Error sensing
strategy is constructed in Section 4. Experiment is carried out in
Section 5. Concluding remarks are summarized in Section 6.

For the location S1, the (0,1,0) cavity mode will be excited resonantly by the point source when the driving frequency reaches its
resonant frequency. The distribution of the cavity sound pressure
will be antisymmetric along the y axis when the (0,1,0) mode is
highly excited. This will result that the average surface impedance
of the active MPPA can not be appropriately adjusted to close to the
characteristic impedance of air. In other words, the surface sound
resistance or reactance will also be antisymmetric along the y axis,
which leads to that the forward sound intensity will be cancelled

3.3. Physical mechanism of active control
The cavity sound field of the active MPPA is controlled by the
point source, which adjusts the air particle velocity in the holes

the active MPPA close to the characteristic impedance of air. This
is the main control mechanism of sound absorption improvement.

6. Conclusions
A more practical large sized active MPPA using point source as
the control source is proposed in this research. The control performance and mechanism of sound absorption improvement are analyzed in detail. Based on these results, the error sensing strategy is
established. Experimental validation is also carried out. The main
conclusions are summarized as follows.
(1) The sound absorption coefficient is highly improved and
close to 1 after control, which validates the feasibility of
the large sized active MPPA. There is a cutoff frequency for
each location of the point source above which the control
effect greatly decreases.
(2) The optimal location of the point source is at the center of
the x-y plane, in which case the maximal cutoff frequency
can be achieved and is the resonant frequency of the
(0,2,0) cavity mode. The (0,1,0), (1,0,0) or (1,1,0) mode will
be excited when the point source is placed at other locations.
Due to the antisymmetric property of these modes, the
sound absorption coefficient can not be improved at or over
the resonant frequencies of these modes, which will be the
cutoff frequency of these locations.
(3) The cavity sound field can be roughly considered as the
plane wave sound filed after control, which indicates that
the PR and IM strategies are also applicative for the large
sized active MPPA. The IM strategy can gain better control
effect since the cavity sound distribution after control for
this strategy is more close to that of the optimal control
state. The cavity sound distribution is more uniform for
the small sized cavity or low frequency excitation, which
can guarantee better control effect in these cases.
