In the present study, the acoustic performance analysis for wedges used in an
anechoic chamber by the boundary element method is conducted. In a wedge
formed by porous material, mechanisms of dissipated processes due to viscosity
and heat conduction are very complicated and are not easy to handle. For
simplicity, the laws of Delany and Bazley are used to replace the porous material
by an equivalent fluid. With this assumption, the agreement between numerical
results and experimental measurements reported elsewhere is still good. Influences
on the pressure reflection coefficient due to the dimensions of wedge, such as the
length of the wedge, the thickness of base, the thickness of air gap, for fiber and
foam materials with different flow resistances are studied. In addition, the hybrid
wedge and the wedge with cutting head are also investigated. © 1997. Published
by Elsevier Science Ltd. All rights reserved.

Experimental measurements are time consuming and
cumbersome, as mentioned above. If a theoretical model
is available for the behavioral analysis of wedges, the
efforts will be lightened. Unfortunately, mechanisms of
acoustic energy dissipation as a result of viscosity and
heat conduction in wedges are very complicated. Therefore, a direct modeling and a theoretical solution for the
wedge is almost impossible. An idea, however, that the
porous material is replaced on a macroscopic scale by
an equivalent fluid4 is useful to approach the problem.
The laws of Delany and Bazley5 give characteristic impedance and complex wave number for sound propagation
in the equivalent fluid. Nevertheless, the expressions
they gave are suitable only for fibrous materials. Similar
empirical expressions for open cell foam materials were
determined by Cummings, 6 and Astley and Cummings 7
through a series of experiments and data analysis. With
this idea, the analysis of wedges with the finite element
method becomes feasible as shown by Easwaran and
Munjal 8.

1 INTRODUCTION
The anechoic chamber is a very important and useful
construction in acoustics and noise control engineering.
There is almost no refection of sound from the wall, and
an acoustically free-field condition can be realized.
Therefore, many experiments, e.g. calibration, electroacoustic transducer testing, noise measurements and
psycho-acoustic testing, needed to be measured in a
free-field can be conducted in these chambers. The most
important factor in anechoic chamber design is to determine the cut-off frequency which strongly depends on the
characteristic of the wedges installed on the wall. In the
past, almost all of the wedges used in anechoic chambers
are based on the charts given by, for example, Beranek
and Sleeper 1 or Koidan et al. 2 The data for these charts
were obtained through a series of experimental measurements. A very long impedance tube is required for the low
frequencies measurement. In spite of this problem, the
experiments are time consuming and cumbersome since
a wide range of the dimensions of the wedge and the densities of porous material should be considered. All of
these experimental data are notably suitable only for
fibrous materials. However, open cell foam materials
are now being used widely in noise control engineering,

5.3. Foam materials
Different kinds of foam materials have been widely used
in recent noise control engineering. In this work, only
the D12 foam material is analyzed and the predicted
results are shown as follows.

5.3.1 Effects of the thickness of base air layer
As mentioned in the previous section, the effects of air
layer and base thickness are similar. Figure 9 shows
the reflection coefficient for wedges with different base
thickness. The effect of air layer thickness (0cm, 10cm
and 20 cm) is plotted in Fig. 10. The influence of increasing base and air layer thickness on cut-off frequency is
not obvious. The overall absorption capability,
however, seems to increase when the thickness is
increase.

5.3.2 Effects of the flow resistance
The coefficients q-Cs, given in Table 1, used in expressing the characteristic impedance and the complex
wave number of D12 foam material are different
from those of fibrous material obviously. However, as
shown in Fig. 11, the pressure reflection characteristic
of the wedge is still similar to that obtained for fibrous
material, as long as the comparison is made in the
range where the reflection is very small. When the
flow resistance reduces, the cut-off frequency is also
reduced. But the trend is more uniform than that of
fibrous material.

5.4 Hybrid wedges
How to reduce the cut-off frequency (i.e. to increase the
available frequency range) is an important consideration
in anechoic chamber design. Increasing the length of
the wedge or reducing the flow resistance of the wedge

109

material are available methods as mentioned above.
Nevertheless, the space of an anechoic chamber is limited
in general and the material with very low resistance may
be too soft to form a wedge in manufacturing. In these
cases, therefore, a hybrid wedge may be another choice.
In Fig. 12, the plot is the pressure reflection characteristics
for three wedges of the same geometry but filled with different materials. It can be seen that the hybrid wedge,
arranged with the larger flow resistance material filled in
the pyramid and the lower flow resistance material in
the base, has a better performance than that of uniform
wedges. In contrast, a reverse arrangement (lower flow
resistance material filled in pyramid and larger flow resistance material in base) in the hybrid wedge has given an
undesired result as shown in Fig. 13.

5.5 Cutting the acute head of the wedge
The acute head of wedges have been cut in some anechoic chamber design. The purpose of the treatment is
not only for increasing the space in anechoic chamber
but also for the convenience in manufacturing. The
influence of the cutting length has been analyzed and
the predicted numerical results are shown in Fig. 14.
When the head length of 10 cm to 20 cm is cut, the reflection characteristic of the wedges changes little and both
wedges are still available for use in anechoic chambers.
But the reflection coefficient is too large (near to 0.1)
for cutting 30 cm of the head length and the wedge is
not suitable for an anechoic chamber.

6 CONCLUSION
A boundary element analysis for the pressure reflection
characteristic of a wedge is presented. The porous material in a wedge is equivalent to a fluid with the laws of
Delany and Bazley. With this assumption, the predicted
numerical results have been verified by those of experimental measurements reported in other publications.
The wedges which are made from fibrous and foam materials are both included and analyzed in the present study.
The cut-off frequency is strongly affected by the
length of the wedge and the flow resistance of the
porous material. If a further reduction in cut-off frequency is required without changing the space of an
anechoic chamber, the hybrid wedge is a good choice.
A proper pressure reflection characteristic is available
through the combination of different materials. Further,
a small percentage cutting of the acute head of the
wedge seems not to affect the performance apparently
and it is still available for use in an anechoic chamber.
Only some design curves, which may be useful for a
designer of anechoic chambers, have been given in the
present work. If the curves are not available, the analysis
method provided can also be adopted to evaluate the
performance easily.
