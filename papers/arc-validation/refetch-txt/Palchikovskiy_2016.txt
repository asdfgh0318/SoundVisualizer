Abstract. The paper presents the results of qualification tests in the new anechoic chamber of Perm National Research
Polytechnic University (PNRPU) built in 2014-2015 and evaluation of the chamber quality in aeroacoustic experiments.
It describes design features of the chamber and its sound-absorption lining. The qualification tests were carried out with
tonal and broadband noise sources in the frequency range 100 Hz – 20 kHz for two different cases of the source
arrangement. In every case, measurements were performed in three directions by traverse microphones. Qualification
tests have determined that in the chamber there is a free acoustic field within radius of 2 m for tonal noise and 3 m for
broadband noise. There was also evaluated acoustic quality of the chamber by measurements of the jet noise and vortex
ring noise. The results of the experiments demonstrate that PNRPU anechoic chamber allows the aeroacoustic
measurements to be performed to obtain quantitative results.

INTRODUCTION
The environmental noise of aircraft is a key issue in assessment of their competitiveness. To satisfy modern and
future noise standards it is necessary to propose novel solutions based on deep scientific research, development of
efficient methods and techniques of noise source identification and suppressing. Experimental studies are a powerful
means for achieving these tasks. Laboratory conditions allow performing experimental studies in the controlled
environment; in addition, they do not require such large financial, time and human resources as full-scale tests.
However, to ensure reliable experimental data in laboratory research it is necessary to provide the free-field acoustic
conditions, i.e. sound propagation as if there are no reflections from the walls. The facility providing such conditions
is called an anechoic chamber.
In 2014-2015, the anechoic chamber has been designed and built in Perm National Research Polytechnic
University (PNRPU) for small-scale experimental studies on jet noise, vortex ring noise and other problems
associated with aeroacoustics. It should be noted that at present the only other analogous operating facility in Russia
is anechoic chamber AC-2 of Central Aerohydrodynamic Institute (TsAGI) built in 1970s, which also enables
experiments with small-scale models.
A new anechoic chamber has to be checked, above all, on ensuring free field conditions. For this purpose, it is
necessary to conduct qualification tests, which give information on the frequency range and dimensions of the free
field region, where deviation of the experimental data from the inverse square law is not above maximum allowable
difference between measured and theoretical free-field levels. In addition, an anechoic chamber has to demonstrate
good quality for measurements in aeroacoustic experiments. It can be proved by detecting the same phenomena,
which have been registered in analogous experiments under conditions of the free field, and observing good
matching of the results. Thus, this work presents the results of the tests, which have been carried out in the new
PNRPU anechoic chamber to demonstrate its capability to be used in aeroacoustic investigations.

DESIGN FEATURES OF THE PNRPU ANECHOIC CHAMBER
The PNRPU anechoic chamber with concrete walls of 40 cm thickness is a well-isolated room from external
noise and vibrations. The walls, ceiling and floor inside the chamber are lined with sound-absorbing wedges from
basalt superfine fibers in acoustically transparent glass cloth. The materials used for the acoustic treatment are
inflammable that ensures fire safety of the chamber. The chamber also ensures requirements on ventilation and
lighting. The chamber floor is rigid, but it can be quickly covered with movable blocks of sound-absorbing wedges
(Fig. 1a). This feature makes it possible to test heavy objects in the chamber and provides convenient access to the
measuring equipment. The specially produced frames with installed wedges allow overlapping the entrance area of
the anechoic chamber to eliminate the reflection effect from the gates (Fig. 1b). To make the chamber easy-to-use
the following additional rooms were designed and built: apparatus room; room for preparing equipment to
experiments; room for fan units of the jet rig (Fig. 1c); special room for vortex ring generator, which is installed into
the wall of the anechoic chamber.

The sound-absorbing wedges have width 20 cm, length 100 cm, and total height 80 cm. The optimal density of
basalt fibers has been chosen in a series of experiments with the wedges in reverberation chambers of TsAGI [1] and
found to be 30 kg/m3. The wedges are assembled in blocks of five pieces inserted into a thin metal wireframe
(diameter of the wire is 2 mm) to preserve the geometrical parameters of the structure.
To determine the frequency lower limit of the wedges the impedance tube has been built. It has a cross section of
0.4 m x 0.4 m and a length of 5.2 m. The transfer function method [2] used in the PNRPU impedance tube has
determined that the absorption coefficient of the wedges with thin metal wireframe is not less than 0.99 for
frequencies higher than 130 Hz. However, the final data on the sound-absorbing capacity of the lining has to be
obtained in qualification tests of the anechoic chamber.

QUALIFICATION TESTS OF THE ANECHOIC CHAMBER
The qualification tests were carried out according to ISO 3745 [3]. They consisted in determination of the
maximum allowable radius between a test source and a measurement location where inverse square law spreading
holds, within some tolerance. This radius determines the region in the anechoic chamber where quantitative acoustic
measurements can be performed without suffering from the reflection of sound from the walls of the chamber, i.e. in
the free-field conditions. The deviation of the measured data from the inverse square law is calculated with the
optimal reference method [4]. The maximum allowable difference in anechoic chambers between measured and
calculated free-field levels according to ISO 3745 is provided in Table 1.

To study the acoustic quality of the anechoic chamber in PNRPU, three sound sources were used:
- low-frequency 15”-loudspeaker (100 - 2000 Hz);
- omnidirectional source Bruel & Kjaer 4295 (low and medium frequencies 125 - 5000 Hz);
- high-frequency sound source based on the compressor driver JBL Selenium D408 Ti (5000 - 20000 Hz).
With these sound sources, two types of experiments were performed. In the first case ( Configuration 1), the source
was placed near a wall, in the region of the expected noise source of a turbulent jet, which will be present in the
chamber. In the second case (Configuration 2), the source was placed in the center of the anechoic chamber, which
corresponds to the typical tests of acoustic characteristics for pieces of machinery.
The measurements in both cases were performed for three radial directions 0 о, 45о and 90о from the source
(Fig. 2). For this purpose the traverse microphone system was constructed in the chamber (Fig. 3). The acoustic data
were obtained by moving microphones in discrete steps along a radial, with the microphones motionless during data
acquisition (30 s) at each microphone location. For all radial directions, the microphones were at least at the distance
of 1 m from the tips of sound-absorbing wedges. Then the source was replaced and the measurements were repeated.

EVALUATION OF THE ANECHOIC CHAMBER QUALITY IN
AEROACOUSTIC EXPERIMENTS
To carry out in the chamber the investigations involving noise generation by aerodynamic sources, the jet rig and
vortex ring generator were designed and built. The jet rig enables flow velocity of up to 200 m/s with nozzle
diameter of 8 cm. Two fans in tandem supply air jet into the chamber and exhaust it from the chamber through the
collector. Each fan has a frequency converter, thus allowing jet velocity be smoothly changed and remotely
controlled with PC software from the apparatus room. The fans are placed outside the chamber in an isolated room

To provide the capability of the chamber for performing measurements of the turbulent jet noise the
measurements were carried out for different velocities of the jet issuing from the nozzle with 40 mm diameter. Fig. 5
demonstrates plots of the power spectral density versus Strouhal number for a microphone at 30° relative to the jet
axis. It has been found that these spectra correspond to the spectral characteristics of small-scale cold jets. Thus, the
obtained results indicate that PNRPU anechoic chamber can be used in aeroacoustic experiments with turbulent jets.

FIGURE 5. Power spectral density of the turbulent jet in PNRPU anechoic chamber.

For research of the vortex ring, which is a fundamental aeroacoustic object, the vortex ring generator was built.
The experiments carried out in the anechoic chamber (Fig. 6) have shown that vortex ring noise can be determined
during the initial path of vortex ring motion even with respect to the background noise of the generator [5]. In the
vortex ring noise spectrum, there is a narrow frequency peak that is typical of vortex ring. Subsequent motion of the
vortex ring leads to shift this peak to lower frequencies. The observed effects are in good agreement to those
obtained earlier in anechoic chamber AC-2 TsAGI [6]. These results demonstrate that PNRPU anechoic chamber
can be used in aeroacoustic experiments with vortex rings.

CONCLUSION
The performed qualification tests have shown that the anechoic chamber in PNRPU realizes the free-field
conditions. The radius of the region where the inverse square law spreading is observed equals to 2 m for tonal noise
source and 3 m for broadband noise. The frequency range in these cases is 125 Hz – 20 kHz. In measurements it is
necessary to provide microphone arrangement at least at the distance of 1 m from the tips of sound-absorbing
wedges.
To provide the capability of the chamber to perform measurements in aeroacoustic investigations, experiments
with jet and vortex ring noise were carried out. It was observed the measurement results for jet noise correspond to
the spectral characteristics of small-scale cold jets. The effects observed in measurements of the vortex rings are the
same as in the known experiments performed in other anechoic chamber.
Thus, obtained results demonstrate that the constructed PNRPU anechoic chamber allows the measurements to be
performed to obtain quantitative results in aeroacoustic investigations.
