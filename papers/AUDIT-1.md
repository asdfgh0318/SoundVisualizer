### Verified facts card

- Bare chamber dimensions: "the length of the walls is 4.46 and 6.96 m, and the height of the ceiling is 3.3 m" (§2, p.2)
- Pyramid absorber geometry/material: "the height of each pyramid is 425 mm, while the thickness of the base is 35 mm, and the width of the base is 150 mm," of "open cell foam rubber saturated with finely-dispersed carbon particles" (§2, p.2)
- Uncovered floor recess: "a recess in the floor of the chamber that is 0.15 m deep, 1.3 m wide and 3.55 m long ... not covered by the absorbing coatings" (§2, p.2)
- Ceiling has non-pyramid patches: "some parts of the ceiling are covered with uniform layers instead of pyramid arrays" (§2, p.2)
- Pyramid geometry matters far more than raw material: "pyramid elements are known to have higher absorption compared to a non-structured layer of a porous material" (§4.1, p.3)
- Directional divergence implicates real scattering, not just source shape: "the maps for deviations ΔLp are not equivalent to each other, which might be an indication of the presence of scattering" (§4.4, p.5)
- Qualification is jointly frequency- and distance-dependent, not a single yes/no: "the chamber meets the requirements only for a limited set of frequencies and distances to the source" (§4.4, p.5)
- Source directivity was assumed, not measured: "the directivity pattern of the source ... was not verified before the measurements. However, the conclusions ... are based on the assumption that the source is omnidirectional" (§4.4, p.5)
- Background noise margin: "the minimal difference is 65.7 dB (within the range 250–5000 Hz)" (§4.2, p.4)
- Low-frequency RT is the weak point: "reverberation time quickly drops from 1.5 s at 63 Hz to 0.21 s at 100 Hz, and for the larger frequencies it remains in-between 0.05 and 0.24 s" (§4.3, p.5)

### Relevance to a small chamber with a rotor on a stand and a vertical microphone arc

The paper's ISO 3745 divergence-law methodology (multi-direction traverses, per-band pass/fail vs. distance, RT and background-noise thresholds) is directly transferable as a qualification template for SoundVisualizer's own chamber, and its finding that directional qualification limits can differ sharply by traverse direction (and that an internal recess/non-uniform coating can be the cause) is a useful cautionary parallel. However, the paper studies a static single point source and fixed mic traverses in an EM-chamber-turned-acoustic-lab, not a rotor-on-a-stand with a fixed vertical mic arc, near-field/extended-source effects, or rotating-source directivity, so it offers no rotor-specific or arc-specific validation — its value here is purely methodological/qualification-procedure, not geometry-specific.

- **Relevance to a small chamber with a rotor on a stand and a vertical microphone arc**: This paper's validated method targets large room-scale hemi/full anechoic chambers (650–800 m³), a different geometry regime, but its central finding — that a usable free field persists below the nominal wedge cutoff within roughly a meter of the chamber's surfaces, because the field is spherical rather than planar there — is directly useful for judging whether a small chamber's short source-to-mic and mic-to-wall distances stay valid below the wedges' rated cutoff.
