### Stephenson 2019, JASA (Letter) — jasa2019_stephenson_recirculation_uas_closed_chambers.txt
- **Facility**: NASA Langley Small Hover Anechoic Chamber (SHAC, formerly Small Anechoic Jet Facility). Fully anechoic mode (inlet/outlet plugged with absorbent material). Dimensions "12.70 × 8.40 × 10.70 (L × W × H) from wedge-tip to wedge-tip" (units ft implied by the companion paper; unclear in this text). Wedge depth, cut-off frequency and qualification standard: not stated. Six 1/4 in. B&K 4939 free-field mics at radii 74.6–89.5 in. from the hub, elevation 0° to −43.6° (Table I, p. 1154). Rotor: 2-blade 9.4 in. fixed-pitch, 5200 RPM, 0.75 lb thrust, BPF ≈ 175 Hz.
- **Problems observed**: Flow recirculation. Spectrogram of M1 (above rotor plane): BPF appears at ~3.5 s at ~45 dB; higher harmonics "not visible between 3.5 and approximately 7.5 s"; "At approximately 7.5 s into the run, flow recirculation in the room has established and the higher harmonics of the BPF are now observable. The amplitudes of the higher harmonic content have increased in excess of 15 dB for many of the harmonics" (p. 1154). Spectra at 5 s vs 8 s: "the first harmonic of the blade passage frequency is relatively unaffected … However, the second harmonic for the out of plane microphones has increased by approximately 30 dB. The third blade passage harmonic has increased by more than 15 dB" (p. 1154). In-plane mic M4 shows "relative steadiness" pre/post recirculation. Mean thrust constant, but "thrust loads are also sensitive to the presence of flow recirculation". Onset "occurred within 5 s of reaching steady state RPM" and is "heavily dependent on the size of the facility, size of the rotor, and the thrust". Unplugging inlet/exhaust made "a negligible difference" (p. 1153).
- **Solutions / mitigations**: Air allowed to settle between runs "for a sufficient period of time to ensure that flow recirculation was not present prior to the start of each run" (p. 1154). Mesh configurations tested "to disrupt the formation of the flow recirculation and attempt to increase the available time to acquire acoustic emissions" — results deferred to a later paper (p. 1155). Recommendation: "Future measurements … must acknowledge recirculation effects and attempt to mitigate the effect on reported measurements" (abstract).
- **Interpretation guidance**: Unsteady-loading noise radiates "predominantly above and below the rotor plane, while thickness noise is predominantly emitted in the plane of the rotor" — so recirculation shows up in out-of-plane mics, not in-plane ones (p. 1154). A sudden appearance of higher BPF harmonics a few seconds after steady RPM, with BPF itself unchanged, is the recirculation signature.
- **Quotable**: "This higher harmonic content is not indicative of what a rotor in hover outdoor would emit, but is instead an artifact of testing within the confined space of an anechoic chamber." (p. 1154) — "It is shown that after flow recirculation is formed, the amplitudes of blade passage frequency harmonics increases in excess of 15 dB." (p. 1155)

### Weitsman 2020, JASA — jasa2020_weitsman_recirculation_rotary_wing.txt
- **Facility**: NASA Langley SHAC, sealed ("inlet and outlet … sealed with an acoustically treated baffle and melamine foam wedges"). "treated with woven fiberglass acoustic wedges, which absorb 99% of incident sound energy above 250 Hz. The interior dimensions (wedge tip to wedge tip) … measure 10.67 ft (3.25 m) in height, 8.38 ft (2.55 m) in width, and 12.67 ft (3.86 m) in length" (p. 1326). Qualification standard: not stated. Six 1/4 in. B&K 4939 mics, nearest at >12 rotor radii ("considered … in the acoustic far-field"). Rotor: DJI 9450 (0.24 m), 5200 RPM, BPF 173 Hz, thrust 3.38 N, hub 1.59 m above floor, rotor plane 1.68 m from the inlet nozzle edge. 20 s records at 80 kHz; spectrogram Δf = 25 Hz, spectra Δf = 5 Hz.
- **Problems observed**: (1) Recirculation: clean region 3.5–7.4 s (3.9 s duration) determined by the standard deviation of the moving mean of filtered thrust exceeding a tolerance of 0.0045; "The BPF harmonics are significantly amplified … with the first three harmonics exhibiting an amplification of nearly 20 dB" (p. 1327); "The broadband noise levels are also amplified, at all three measurement locations" (p. 1329); thrust envelope modulates and RPM fluctuates while mean thrust stays 3.38 N; torque shows "no distinct indications". Tonal extraction shows BPF unchanged; amplification "attributed to unsteady loading" (p. 1329). Power coefficient decreases after recirculation "resembles the performance of a rotor operating in ground effect" (p. 1334). (2) Short clean window → large random uncertainty: 2 s average gives "autospectral random uncertainty of εr = 22.80%, which results in a random SPL uncertainty of ur,SPL ∈ {−1.12, +0.89} dB" (p. 1328). (3) Motor tones at 4.9 and 7.3 kHz (8th and 12th harmonic of supply line frequency) (p. 1328). (4) Facility noise floor: in-plane higher-order extracted harmonics "fall below the narrowband data … since the narrowband signal in this frequency range is close to the noise floor of the test facility" (p. 1328). (5) Mesh holders reflect: "tonal noise components at frequencies above 2 kHz are slightly amplified. This is thought to be attributed to acoustic reflections off the mesh holder apparatus" (p. 1333).
- **Solutions / mitigations**: Quiescent air before each run; fast ramp-up (3.5 s). Six PETP woven screen meshes (36–72% open area, Table II, p. 1327) in five configurations (Fig. 5). Upstream mesh (1 D ahead): ineffective, clean region ~3 s, and "the upstream mesh distorts the inflow" — BPF harmonics amplified even before recirculation (p. 1331). Ducted and orthogonal meshes: "considerably alters the inflow conditions"; orthogonal attenuates BPF ~3 dB and amplifies higher harmonics (p. 1331–1332). Single downstream #3 mesh at 6 D: clean region 6.25 s vs 3.9 s (p. 1332). Dual downstream meshes (#4 at 3.3 D, #3 at 6 D): clean region ~10 s; uncertainty falls to εr = 11.85%, ur,SPL ∈ {−0.55, +0.49} dB (p. 1333); 3rd/4th harmonics attenuated ~5 dB relative to baseline; mean CT, CP within baseline uncertainty. Suggests upholstering mesh holders in absorbent material. Recommends flow visualization to find optimum mesh placement and a generalized model for onset time (p. 1335).
- **Interpretation guidance**: Track the standard deviation of filtered thrust to delimit the clean window; recirculation shows as amplified BPF harmonics + modulated thrust envelope + RPM fluctuation, greatest out of plane; "The greatest tonal amplification is evident outside of the rotor plane" (p. 1329). "Sequential amplification" — higher harmonics amplify before the 2nd/3rd with meshes — "attributed to the break up of coherent structures, which are of the length scales that influence the amplitudes of the first several BPF harmonics" (p. 1332). Comparing tonal-extracted (per-rev averaged) vs narrowband levels separates steady from unsteady loading contributions.
- **Quotable**: "Recirculation is an artifact of operating rotary-wing systems in an enclosed facility, and occurs when the rotor wake propagates throughout the test facility and is subsequently ingested by the rotor." (p. 1335) — "This exemplifies how sensitive the acoustic emissions of a lightly loaded rotor, operating in hover, are to slight variations in the inflow conditions." (p. 1331) — "Two meshes positioned parallel to, and aft of the rotor plane, yielded the best results, extending the duration prior to the onset of recirculation to approximately 10 s." (p. 1335)

### Nardari 2019, AIAA Aeroacoustics (2019-2497) — aiaa2019_nardari_flow_confinement_uav_rotor.txt
- **Facility**: Amazon Prime Air hemi-anechoic chamber: "Lx = 8.76D long, Ly = 8.86D wide and Lz = 7.79D tall, where D is the rotor diameter. Its walls and ceiling are treated with anechoic foam paneling, while the floor – a thick concrete slab – is left untreated and reflective" (p. 2). Absolute D not stated. Qualification (in-house): "hemi-anechoic within ±1 dB above a blade passing frequency (BPF) harmonic of 2.2, but only within ±4 dB below that, primarily due to the onset of standing waves, which are a consequence of the nearly cuboidal shape of the chamber" (p. 2). Rotor on a pole 3.34 D above the floor, thrust directed downward; 10 GRAS 40AE 1/2 in. mics; 5 s records.
- **Problems observed**: (1) Standing waves below ~2.2×BPF (above). Measurement error "approximately ±2 dB above a BPF harmonic of 2.2 and ±4 dB below that for any one 1/3-Octave band" (p. 3), obtained by comparing a loudspeaker's recorded level at each mic with inverse-distance attenuation. (2) Recirculation (LBM confined vs unconfined): "the effect of the flow confinement results in an almost constant offset of about 5 dBA, with the largest differences occurring at microphones close to the rotor axis" (p. 6); first BPF tone unchanged; "starting from the third BPF harmonic, the tones for the confined results are about 5 to 10 dB higher"; "Along the rotor axis … the confined case is up to 9 dB louder … both towards the sky (0 deg) and the ground (180 deg)" while only ~2 dB at the max radiation angle ~120° (p. 11); high-frequency self-noise (>30×BPF) insensitive; BVI negligible. Confined simulation needed 990 revolutions to converge vs 18 unconfined (p. 5–6). (3) Floor reflection: "acoustic waves generated by the rotor and reflected by the floor can be seen traveling from the floor to the ceiling of the chamber" (p. 6). (4) First three BPF harmonics under-predicted in the confined sim — possibly large eddies needing longer sampling, wall-treatment details, or rig vibration (p. 7–8).
- **Solutions / mitigations**: Mics "positioned in such a way as to minimize noise from flow over them (wind-screens are also used) and any effects from standing waves in the chamber" (p. 3). Loudspeaker inverse-square check per mic for error budget. Modelling the chamber with an acoustic porous medium (APM) that "absorbs the impinging acoustic waves but preserves the confinement effect" (p. 3) to reproduce the in-chamber measurement. The authors argue that an untreated closed chamber is a *relevant* (turbulent-inflow-like) test condition and want models that account for it rather than removing it (p. 2).
- **Interpretation guidance**: Excess in BPF harmonics ≥3 with an unchanged fundamental, largest on the rotor axis, is the confinement/unsteady-loading signature; leading-edge interaction at ~80% span is the mechanism (p. 12–13). Below ~2.2×BPF, ±4 dB scatter is a room-mode artefact, not source behaviour.
- **Quotable**: "Our calibration experiments indicate that the chamber is hemi-anechoic within ±1 dB above a blade passing frequency (BPF) harmonic of 2.2, but only within ±4 dB below that, primarily due to the onset of standing waves, which are a consequence of the nearly cuboidal shape of the chamber." (p. 2) — "It was found that flow recirculation leads to a nearly 5 dBA increase in OASPL across most microphone locations, but has negligible impact on the thrust and torque of the rotor." (p. 16)

### Ma 2024, Acoustics Australia — acoustaust2024_ma_recirculation_free_flying_uas.txt
- **Facility**: HKUST Aerodynamic and Acoustic Facility anechoic chamber, "wedge tip-to-tip dimensions of 8.1 m (L) × 6 m (W) × 5.1 m (H) and a cut-off frequency of 100 Hz" (p. 314). Qualification standard: not stated. Five 1/4 in. GRAS 46BE mics with windscreens in a vertical plane; free-flying DJI Phantom 4 Pro (four 240 mm rotors, 1.4 kg) hovering at 2.2–3.2 m; 60 s records at 50 kHz.
- **Problems observed**: Recirculation forms "around 30 s after the UAS's take-off, manifested as prominent fluctuations in blade passage frequency and its harmonics" (abstract); "the precise determination of this transition moment is not feasible as the intensity of the tonal fluctuations increases gradually" (p. 316). Instantaneous OASPL (100–20 000 Hz) "does not show a significant deviation after the recirculation develops but presents slight higher fluctuation values up to 2 dB" (p. 316). Post-recirculation, "tonal spikes become broader, and two or more separated spikes can be present at high-order BPF harmonics", peak values decrease (p. 317). Table 2: tonal SPL changes −0.89…+0.66 dB; broadband OASPL +1.08…+2.03 dB (largest at mic #5, out of plane); spectral entropy +0.068…0.083 (12–13%) (p. 318). Broadband increment grows with hover height (closer to ceiling → less uniform, non-axial recirculated flow) and falls with gross mass (inertia) (p. 319–321). Contrast with isolated-rotor studies attributed to a chamber ~8× (Stephenson) and ~12× (Bu) larger by volume, rotor–airframe interaction tones dominating, and rotor-speed fluctuations dispersing energy around the harmonics (p. 317).
- **Solutions / mitigations**: Test protocol: start recording at idle, climb in <3 s, hover for the 60 s window, land, "Wait for at least 5 min to ensure an environment with quiescent air before the next test" (p. 314–315). Spectral entropy (100–2000 Hz band, Eq. 2) proposed "as a reference to detect the formation of the recirculation" (p. 316). Broadband extracted by moving-median + moving-minimum filtering then Gaussian smoothing; tonal SPL by narrow-band integration of the band ≥ −20 dB re peak (p. 315). Four repeats with the UAS heading rotated front/left/back/right.
- **Interpretation guidance**: For a free-flying multirotor, recirculation "does not significantly change the total acoustic energy but increases the uncertainties in the spectral distribution" — look for broadened/split BPF harmonics and rising spectral entropy rather than an OASPL step (abstract, p. 316). "the instantaneous OASPL value cannot distinguish the transition into the post-recirculation flow regime" (p. 316).
- **Quotable**: "the recirculation has a negligible effect on the tonal SPLs at the first four BPF harmonics but slightly (∼2 dB) enhances the broadband OASPL out of the rotor plane." (p. 321) — "a characterization of the flow recirculation effect before conducting any accurate acoustic assessments is important for UAS noise assessments in closed facilities." (p. 321)

### Ma 2022, Quiet Drones (HKUST) — quietdrones2022_ma_hemianechoic_multirotor_measurement.txt
- **Facility**: Same HKUST chamber: "wedge-to-wedge dimension of 8.1 m (L) × 6 m (W) × 5.1 m (H) and a cut-off frequency of 100 Hz in the full anechoic configuration. With the bottom wedges removed, the chamber is in the hemi-anechoic configuration and the height is 5.7 m" (p. 2). Qualification standard: not stated. 15 GRAS 46BE 1/4 in. mics (9 vertical near a corner, 6 horizontal 0.12 m above bottom wedges, 0.5 m spacing), windscreens; optical motion capture; 20 s at 50 kHz, Welch Δf = 5 Hz.
- **Problems observed**: (1) Hemi-anechoic ground reflection: OASPL decay vs distance "does not agree well with the spherical decay reference, and the deviations can reach as large as 3 dB", while repeatability error bars are ~0.5 dB in both configurations, so "the discrepancy in the hemi-anechoic results is attributed to the presence of the reflective surface" (p. 8). Two adjacent near-ground mics (#9, #10) differ strongly in the low-to-mid range: "At the BPF … the difference in the measured SPL can be as significant as 10 dB" in hemi-anechoic, "little difference" in full anechoic (p. 8). (2) Far-field: proposes reference dimension L = d + D (0.59 m for this drone) and criterion R ≥ 5L; full-anechoic decay follows 1/R over 3.4L–7.4L (p. 3–4, 8). (3) EU 2019/945 test code (hover 0.5 m over a reflecting plane) criticised: rotor wake "can directly impinge the reflecting surface, resulting in an aerodynamic ground effect" and no far-field distance requirement (p. 2). (4) Cruise fly-over produced "a prominent pressure fluctuation at low frequencies due to rotor wake" at the mic (p. 12).
- **Solutions / mitigations**: Use the full anechoic configuration for spectra ("The results illustrate the need to use a full anechoic facility to measure the drone noise spectra", p. 8). Position tracking to verify hover drift (0.05 m horizontal, 0.026 m vertical); five repeats; scale data to a uniform distance (3.2 m) once 1/R decay is verified; rotate the drone heading in 15° steps to sample azimuth with a fixed array; swap rotors diagonally between repeats.
- **Interpretation guidance**: Compare adjacent microphones — if they disagree by several dB in the low-mid range with the same distance/angle, suspect the reflecting plane. A level-vs-log-distance slope departing from the 1/R line by up to 3 dB in the hemi-anechoic case is a reflection artefact, not source behaviour.
- **Quotable**: "the acoustic interference caused by ground reflection can deviate from the measured spectra, especially at low frequencies. Thus, using a hemi-anechoic chamber could considerably degrade the tonal noise assessment accuracy." (p. 12)

### Kim 2022, ICSV28 (file named quietdrones2022_kim…) — quietdrones2022_kim_drone_noise_vs_test_environment.txt
- **Facility**: "box-type semi-anechoic room, which was in Daejeon, South Korea … inner dimension was 8.2 m in length, 7.0 m in width and 4 m in height from the metallic ground surfaces. Cut-off frequency of the room was 63 Hz." Background "< 15 dB(A)". Outdoor site: artificial-grass football field 101 × 62 m, background ~50 dB(A) (p. 2). Wedge depth/qualification standard: not stated. Method: EU 2019/945 (ISO 3744) replaced by Directive 2000/14/EC six-mic hemisphere (4 mics at 1.5 m, 2 at 2.4 m), radius 3 m indoors, 5 m outdoors; hover at 0.5 m; A-weighted sound power (p. 2–3).
- **Problems observed**: The top microphone of the ISO 3744 ten-position hemisphere risks collision with "unstably hovering drones" (p. 2). Upper mics M5/M6 (2.4 m) read "higher sound levels by about 3 dB on average than other four microphones at 1.5 m height. This can be seen as the effect of the propeller wind acting in a downward direction" — LWA from the four 1.5 m mics is 0.9–1.9 dB lower than from six (p. 4). Four-point simplified LWA at 0.5 m height is 4.6–7.4 dB lower than Directive method, attributed to "the measurement height rather than … the number of measurement points" (p. 4). Outdoors: quadcopter only ~10 dB above background; levels rise ~1 dB (octocopter) and ~3.5 dB (Phantom 4 Pro) when hover height goes 0.5 → 5 m at 1.5 m mic height (p. 6–7). Safeguard gives inconsistent ±0.5–2.7 dB changes.
- **Solutions / mitigations**: Six-position Directive 2000/14/EC layout to avoid the overhead mic; windshields outdoors; direct comparison: LWA 82.6 dB (hover 0.5 m, semi-anechoic) vs 82.5 dB (hover 1 m, outdoor) → "it may be possible to measure drone noise as much as a laboratory even outdoors if only the difference from the background noise can be sufficiently secured" (p. 7).
- **Interpretation guidance**: Mics above the rotor plane in the downwash read high (~3 dB) — treat as flow, not acoustic, contribution (p. 4). No explicit reflection/standing-wave interpretation given.
- **Quotable**: "two microphones of M5 and M6 at 2.4 m height among the six microphones shows higher sound levels by about 3 dB on average than other four microphones at 1.5 m height. This can be seen as the effect of the propeller wind acting in a downward direction." (p. 4)

### Rasmussen 2022, Quiet Drones (GRAS) — quietdrones2022_rasmussen_accurate_drone_noise_measurement.txt
- **Facility**: No specific chamber; analytical/BEM (COMSOL) study of a microphone over a reflecting plane vs ground-board/flush mounting for outdoor and semi-anechoic measurements. Notes anechoic chambers "may be too small [for larger UAS] and may incur problems with recirculation and safety" (p. 1–2).
- **Problems observed**: Direct–reflected interference at a 1.2 m pole mic with the source overhead (hover) "is like a comb filter, with the first minimum at the frequency where the microphone height 1.2 m equals ¼ of the wavelength: f0 = c/(4l) ≈ 71.4 Hz" and first maximum "f1 = c/(2l) ≈ 143 Hz" (p. 3). In 1/3-octave: "frequencies coincident with the comb-filter minima are reduced by more than 20 dB, and the frequencies coincident with the comb-filter maxima are amplified by 6 dB" (p. 4). Overall level at 1.2 m "will be 3 dB higher than the corresponding level measured in a full free field" (p. 4). Pure tones at 72 Hz + harmonics: some "+6dB by comb filter", some "Removed by comb filter" (Fig. 6, p. 5) — cannot be corrected. Soft ground (30 kPa·s/m² flow resistivity) still gives "considerable" interference at 1.2 m; lowering the mic "will further increase the influence" (p. 6). Hover (source overhead) is "the worst" case; for fly-by the comb pattern shifts with elevation angle (Fig. 9, p. 7). Inverted mic over ICAO ground plate: "very sensitive to changes in the distance between the ground plate and the microphone diaphragm" (7 vs 4 vs 9.6 mm, Fig. 13) and "disturbs the measurements above 10 kHz" (p. 9); plate centre "is not the optimum position" vs r = 0.15 m (p. 8–9).
- **Solutions / mitigations**: Broadband sources can in principle be corrected for comb filtering, tones cannot (p. 5). Ground board with **flush-mounted** microphone: reflection "becomes well defined and will result in a simple pressure doubling" (+6 dB), usable to 20 kHz for tonal and broadband sources, stationary or moving (p. 10–11). Full anechoic chamber for the full spectral content where feasible.
- **Interpretation guidance**: Dip spacing ↔ path length: first dip at c/(4h) for source overhead, maxima at c/(2h); dips deepen to >20 dB and are smoothed only at high frequency by 1/3-octave filtering; +3 dB overall for a pole mic over a hard plane, +6 dB for flush mounting; "the specific circumstances of the actual measurement" must be considered before comparing levels (p. 4).
- **Quotable**: "The interference of the direct wave and the reflected wave at the microphone position is like a comb filter, with the first minimum at the frequency where the microphone height 1.2 m equals ¼ of the wavelength" (p. 3) — "if the source signal contains pure tone components, it will not be possible to do this correction." (p. 5)

### Hochbaum 2026, conference paper (session "UAS/UAM noise modeling"; venue unclear in text) — hochbaum2026_drone_directivity_anechoic_realistic_flight.txt
- **Facility**: "fully anechoic room of the Engineering Acoustics Lab at TU Berlin, with dimensions of 13.5 m, 8.3 m, and 7.4 m (L × W × H) and a lower cut-off frequency of 63 Hz"; walkable Kevlar mesh floor; heavy-duty safety nets; usable flight volume ~10 × 6 × 3.5 m (p. 3). Wedge depth and qualification standard: not stated. 64 GRAS 40PK 1/4 in. mics at 102.4 kHz; LiDAR tracking of free-flying X500 quadcopter (1.96 kg) and UBADRON hexacopter (12 kg, 2.6 m tip-to-tip).
- **Problems observed**: (1) Wind-induced low-frequency noise on mics under the drones despite custom windscreens: blocks rejected if any FFT bin exceeded 70 dB below 100 Hz (X500) or 77 dB below 40 Hz (UBADRON); "For the X500 55.2% and for UBADRON 56.4% of the checked blocks were rejected" (Fig. 4, p. 6). "Owing to the UAV's size and the resulting rotor downwash, no valid measurements were obtained below the UAV" (p. 9). (2) Below cut-off: "the BPF band of the UBADRON partly falls below the cut-off frequency of the anechoic chamber" (63 Hz band) (p. 9). (3) Irregular angular sampling and residual source motion; "A residual uncertainty in the exact temporal alignment remains" (p. 4). (4) Limited flight distance: manoeuvres "not fully stationary in speed" (p. 7). Recirculation: not mentioned.
- **Solutions / mitigations**: Backpropagation to r0 = 1 m assuming a point source (0.1 s blocks, 50% overlap); equiangular angular binning (Δθ = 10°) with energetic averaging; empirical wind-rejection criterion; longer 1 s windows for psychoacoustic metrics; de-Dopplerisation for fly-throughs; recommend increasing mic distance below the drone to reduce wind overload (p. 11).
- **Interpretation guidance**: Regions with few valid observations (245°–295° below the drones, ~65° for UBADRON) show "uncertainties in the radiation pattern … attributed to the low number of valid observations and to possible outliers or wind-induced disturbances" (p. 9) — i.e. treat sparsely sampled polar bins as unreliable, not as lobes.
- **Quotable**: "Owing to the UAV's size and the resulting rotor downwash, no valid measurements were obtained below the UAV." (p. 9) — "It should be noted that the BPF band of the UBADRON partly falls below the cut-off frequency of the anechoic chamber." (p. 9)

### Gallo 2025, Acta Acustica — actaacustica2025_gallo_aeroacoustic_test_bench.txt
- **Facility**: VKI ALCOVES anechoic laboratory: main test room 2.5 m × 4.5 m plus a discharge room behind a wall partition with a 1 m × 1 m opening; "commissioned according to the ISO:3745 standard [22], demonstrating free-field behavior down to 150 Hz for both broadband and tonal noise sources [23]" (p. 2) (the reference list gives [22] as ISO 3744 — inconsistency in text). Elsewhere "the range of frequencies for which the room is practically anechoic (f > 100 Hz)" (p. 6). Propeller axis aligned with the partition opening, propeller plane 1.80 m from it, auxiliary fan not run. GRAS 40PL mics; 3-mic initial set plus a 24-mic traversing antenna (216 points); 30 s records at 51.2 kHz.
- **Problems observed**: Background: "The cooling system defines the background broadband noise for frequencies above 300 Hz. The motors add a tonal background noise"; peaks at the 100 Hz shaft frequency "presumably linked to slight asymmetries or misalignment" (p. 6). Motor-to-propeller margin at 1st BPF > 30 dB. Recirculation: spectrogram over 30 s "does not reveal any rise in either tonal or broadband noise levels over time. Given the small size of the room, one would have expected to see a transient effect" — attributed either to wake turbulence "trapped" in the downstream room or to the recirculation time being comparable to the 10 s RPM-stabilisation ramp, "in which case discriminating both transient effects becomes difficult" (p. 7). Cooling air injection offsets the load cell (p. 3). Coaxial phase not locked → repeatability checked over 10 s at one mic (Appendix B). Reflections/standing waves: not discussed.
- **Solutions / mitigations**: Direct the wake through the partition opening into a discharge room; axisymmetric fairing to minimise strut interaction; background/cooling/motor-only spectra recorded as commissioning steps; 30 s acquisition "according to the statistical convergence of the signal"; distance scaling SPL2 = SPL1 − 20 log(r2/r1) for cross-facility comparison with Weitsman et al. (p. 6–7).
- **Interpretation guidance**: Use a spectrogram to look for a time-dependent rise in tones/broadband as the recirculation indicator; if the ramp-up is as long as the expected onset, the two transients cannot be separated (p. 7).
- **Quotable**: "The spectrum does not reveal any rise in either tonal or broadband noise levels over time. Given the small size of the room, one would have expected to see a transient effect of the recirculating flow." (p. 7)

### Alkmim 2022, JASA — jasa2022_alkmim_hemispherical_array_drone_directivity.txt
- **Facility**: "hemi-anechoic chamber with an acoustically hard ground … room interior dimensions are 16 × 12.5 × 6 m (length × width × height) with fiberglass wedges covered by perforated aluminum panels" (p. 2736). Cut-off/qualification standard: not stated. Drone (MikroKopter quad, 2.5 kg) rigidly fixed at 1.57 m height; 18-mic hemispherical array (GRAS 40-PH, 1/4 in.) of average radius 0.98 ± 0.10 m; binaural head at 4 m.
- **Problems observed**: Ground reflection "minimized during measurements but not fully suppressed, especially at low frequencies" (p. 2737). Mic 1 directly under the rotors needed a windscreen; "At low frequencies, below 50 Hz, the −70° direction has much higher levels compared to the other directions, which can be attributed to the downward airflow across the microphone diaphragm" (p. 2742); windscreen "can account for high-frequency attenuation at this position". Spatial aliasing above fmax = 163.77 Hz for 3rd-order SHD (R = 1 m) — valid only up to 1st BPF below 4900 rpm (p. 2739). Polar "null point … closer to the 90° elevation" and up to 12 dB elevation variation (p. 2740); nulls "attributed to the symmetry of the drone". OSPL of reconstructed vs target differs by 2.5 dB at 4987 rpm (p. 2743). Recirculation: not mentioned.
- **Solutions / mitigations**: "a 2.5 × 2.5 m² area under the UAS was covered by a foam material with approximately 0.07 m thickness to mitigate ground reflections" (p. 2736); band-pass 20 Hz–20 kHz "to discard flow-induced low-frequency pseudo-sound" (p. 2741); yz-plane symmetry assumption to complete the sphere; more sensors recommended to raise the aliasing limit.
- **Interpretation guidance**: Very-low-frequency excess at the mic beneath the rotor is flow pseudo-sound, not radiation. SHD errors from imprecise mic position, limited sensors and truncation (p. 2740).
- **Quotable**: "Additionally, a 2.5 × 2.5 m² area under the UAS was covered by a foam material with approximately 0.07 m thickness to mitigate ground reflections." ### Fasulo 2025, Aerospace (MDPI) 12:647 — aerospace2025_rotor_noise_directivity_decay_configurations.txt
- **Facility**: CIRA semi-anechoic chamber, "with a 90 Hz cut-off frequency, measures 5.65 m × 4.45 m × 4.00 m" (p. 3); floor "treated as an acoustically rigid surface" (p. 10). Wedge depth / qualification standard: not stated. Ten 1/2 in. - **Problems observed**: (1) Floor reflection distorts directivity: BPF-1 patterns "depart markedly from the ideal distribution … dips are far less pronounced than theory anticipates. Ongoing numerical simulations … indicate that reflections from the chamber floor redirect tonal energy into directions that should otherwise be relatively quiet, most notably at 0° and 180°" (p. 12). Quadcopter BPF-1 directivity smooths at high rpm "likely … from ground reflections that become more energetic as rotor loading grows" (p. 22). (2) Distance decay: L < 2D ~17 dB/doubling (reactive near field); beyond 3D "marginally less than 6 dB, consistent with the influence of ground reflections. Superimposed on this trend, pronounced oscillations appear, most evident at 7000 and 8000 rpm. These fluctuations originate from constructive and destructive interference between the direct acoustic wave and its ground-reflected counterpart, yielding frequency- and range-dependent local sound pressure minima and maxima" (p. 15); "The quasi-constant OASPL observed at certain distances and frequencies is not yet understood". (3) Support pylon shielding: pylon offset near the 30° mic "produces additional shading"; reversing rotation sense changes OASPL by ~1 dB (3000–5000 rpm), ~3 dB (6000), 3–4 dB (7000–8000), SD up to ~4 dB; "The support pylon lies directly on the acoustic line of sight of microphone 10, producing shielding" (p. 11–12). (4) Plate installation: left/right asymmetry with mean 6.5 dB at BPF-1 between mirrored mics, ~10 dB local at BPF-2 in the rotor plane; blockage and 1.6 dB path-length effects insufficient → "near-field interactions, in which the rotor's pressure field couples with the plate edge and ground surface" (p. 22). (5) Mic 9 in the downwash: "intense, large-scale turbulent structures that amplified the low-frequency broadband noise" — omitted from OASPL (p. 9–10). (6) Motor tones at fmot (133 Hz), electromagnetic ~1870 Hz with ±q·fmot sidebands (rotor eccentricity), and rpm-invariant peaks "likely associated with structural resonances" (p. 9–10); motor tone ≥ 20 dB below BPF, raises OASPL ≤ 0.004 dB. (7) Multi-rotor BPF lines split/broaden with unequal rpm; Δf = 3 Hz chosen so they "coalesce into a single, unambiguous peak" (p. 7). Recirculation: ruled out — spectrograms over 30 s show "BPF harmonics remain sharp and strictly horizontal … no progressive rise in broadband energy"; rotor plane ~6 D above floor, flow parallel to floor, nearest wall along the wake axis > 12 D (p. 18).
- **Solutions / mitigations**: OASPL lower bound at the chamber cut-off; omit the in-wake mic from OASPL; exclude the 0°–90° sector where the pylon interferes (p. 19); motor-only runs to identify motor tones; repeat with two blade sets (mean difference < 0.6 dB) and both rotation senses to separate installation asymmetry from source; spectrogram check for recirculation; a numerical study planned to isolate floor/pylon effects.
- **Interpretation guidance**: Ripples/plateaus in level-vs-distance in the far field are direct–ground interference (p. 15). Filled-in theoretical nulls at 0°/180° indicate floor reflection. Left–right asymmetry that flips with rotation sense indicates structural/environmental asymmetry: "rotation sense can dominate the apparent non-uniformity of the acoustic field whenever structural or environmental asymmetries are present" (p. 12). Broadened BPF clusters = unequal rotor speeds, not a room effect.
- **Quotable**: "the ground was treated as an acoustically rigid surface, so local reflections could either reinforce or attenuate the radiated sound, thereby altering the apparent directivity." (p. 10) — "Reversing the sense of rotation revealed that even modest geometric asymmetries, here the support pylon, can shift directivity by up to 4 dB." (p. 24)

This acoustic treatment ensures anechoic facility characteristics down to a cut-on frequency of approximately 200 Hz" (p. 2). 28-element linear array of B&K 4939 mics along an upper corner, "an average distance of 0.49 m from the acoustic wedge tips" (p. 16). Comparison facility: SALT anechoic chamber, five mics at 10R. Qualification standard: not stated.
- **Problems observed**: (1) Below cut-on: BPF directivity vs PAS prediction agrees within ±2.5 dB at 5400/6000 RPM (BPF 180/200 Hz) but "exceeds 5 dB at an elevation angle of θ = −47.75°" at 4800 RPM (BPF 160 Hz): "This may be due to reflections from the wall-mounted wedges at this frequency" (p. 16). Treating the 0.49 m mic–wedge-tip distance as λ/4 "yields a notional cut-on frequency of 175 Hz" (p. 16). (2) Test-cell recirculation of the open jet: nozzle/collector area ratio rose 0.35 → 0.50, thicker shear layer, so "a considerable portion of the open jet flow [can] escape the collector and recirculate in the test cell" (p. 7); empty-tunnel spectra show a broad hump 500–800 Hz at collector ΔX = 0 and, for collector moved −229/−305 mm, "a prominent increase in broadband noise across 100 ≤ f ≤ 250 Hz" at the downstream mic (p. 7). (3) Flow gusts: higher LSAWT tonal levels at θ = −45° "believed to be related to the presence of occasional flow gusts experienced within the test cell, the wind tunnel inlet and exhaust of which were open" (p. 15). (4) Turbulence intensity rises axially/laterally (Table 3, up to 1.61%) — restricts usable core for multi-rotor articles (p. 6). (5) Foam windscreen amplitude deviations above 15 kHz (p. 8).
- **Solutions / mitigations**: Rotor mounted in propeller orientation so the wake convects into the diffuser, "reduced the possibility of measurement contamination due to wake flow recirculation" (p. 13); rotor plane ~5R upstream of the fairing; collector moved upstream ~152 mm as a lower-noise setting; experimental acoustically-transparent windscreen giving "7 to 20 dB" reduction of recirculation-induced self-noise over 100–1500 Hz (p. 8); far-field distance correction (spherical spreading) to compare facilities; 5 min settle between runs for load-cell tare; five-second block averages with ur,SPL = ±0.6 dB (p. 14).
- **Interpretation guidance**: Quarter-wavelength of the microphone-to-wedge-tip distance sets a practical low-frequency limit — "caution should be used in quantitative comparisons at frequencies below the notional cut-on frequency of approximately 175 Hz", even though directivity *trends* below it may still look right (p. 16). Directional discrepancy at one elevation at the lowest BPF is a wedge-reflection artefact.
- **Quotable**: "It is important to note that the LSAWT microphones are mounted an average distance of 0.49 m from the acoustic wedge tips … Treating this as a 1/4-wavelength yields a notional cut-on frequency of 175 Hz." (p. 16)

### Whelchel 2023, PhD thesis (Virginia Tech) — whelchel2023_phd_suas_rotor_noise_outdoor_lab.txt
Read in full: abstract, Sec. 1.3.2 (facility limitations), Ch. 3 Secs. 3.1–3.6 (facilities), Sec. 4.1 (hover/flyover, ground-board study), Sec. 5.1 (facility effects), Sec. 5.4 (p. 122), Sec. 5.4 open-jet limits (p. 131), Ch. 7 conclusions; remainder skimmed.
- **Facilities**: (a) Virginia Tech anechoic chamber (Eckel): "fully anechoic, providing a low frequency cut-off of 100 Hz, and has inner dimensions from wedge tip to wedge tip of 4.5 (L) × 2.5 (W) × 2.5 (H) m" (p. 41); qualification standard not stated. Rotor on a wire-tensioned stand; 11 mics, line array 1.1 m lateral, mic 2 farthest out of plane (72°), r = 1.10–3.59 m (Table 3.4, p. 44). (b) Virginia Tech Anechoic Open Jet: 0.762 × 0.762 m test section, "fully anechoic down to 420 Hz", 40 m/s max (p. 53); elsewhere "anechoic limit of the facility of 400 Hz" (p. 92). (c) Drone Park (netted outdoor, 91.4 × 36.6 × 25.9 m) with mics at the centre of 1 m diameter, 1.3 cm HPVA plywood ground plates per IEC 61400-11 (p. 38). (d) KEAS asphalt airstrip. Rotors: DJI 9450S (BPF ≈ 175 Hz) and 2170R (533 mm, BPF ≈ 90–92 Hz, "just below the anechoic limit of the chamber, 100 Hz", p. 84).
- **Problems observed**:
  - *Recirculation in the chamber* (Sec. 5.1.1): no clear onset — "The BPF and its harmonics were shown to fluctuate in amplitude with time but do not reveal a clear instant when recirculation starts to occur like observed in the results of Stephenson et al." (p. 81). Harmonic levels vs FFT averaging time (2–28 s): "The BPF increases by nearly 3 dB between the 2 s and 4 s sample window … The BPF and its harmonics are all shown to increase by at least 7 dB with increasing averaging time suggesting that the turbulent wake is being reingested … converging towards a steady state after approximately 20s" (p. 82). Abstract/conclusions: "recirculation effects were shown to increase harmonic amplitudes more than 8 dB" (abstract; p. 144). Directivity: mic 2 (near rotor axis) OASPL +1.6 dB and BPF SPL +6.2 dB for the 28 s vs 4 s window; "measurements in the anechoic chamber, regardless of averaging periods, are consistent in plane but differ as the observer position moves around toward the axis of rotation" (p. 89). Higher-harmonic SPL standard deviations 4.6–6.9 dB in the chamber vs 3.9–5.2 dB outdoors (Table 5.1, p. 92). Chamber BPF > 5 dB above other facilities for the 9450S; 10 dB for the 2170R (p. 97–98).
  - *Screens ineffective*: two 1524 mm varying-solidity screens (69% open/2.7 mm, 59% open/1.7 mm) downstream, spaced 4 or 7 radii (9450S) / 4 or 6 radii (2170R) (p. 41): "None of the results showed a clear delay like observed by Whiteside et al. … It is likely that an effective screen configuration is rotor and anechoic chamber dependent" (p. 84); for the 2170R "the screens have no beneficial effect on recirculation and turbulence ingestion" (p. 85).
  - *Open jet with rotor as large as the inlet*: "The limits of the facility are being exceeded when measuring the noise at the BPF of 68 Hz for these larger rotors as the anechoic limit of the open jet section of 420 Hz and the size of the open jet inlet is 30". Constructive and destructive interference could be resultant of the measured noise reflecting off the test section walls and inside of the inlet"; blade tips interact with the shear layer; "Trough and hump like features would appear in the measured directivities that would change location with RPM" (p. 131). For the 2170R the open jet gave higher tones than the chamber — "significant recirculation is likely to occur" in the smaller test section (p. 99).
  - *Ground-board interference outdoors* (Sec. 4.1.2): mid-frequency "lumps from 1 kHz and 3 kHz spaced approximately 600 Hz apart" under the hovering hexacopter; method of images for a mic 32.5 mm above the plate predicts nulls at 5190 and 15660 Hz, so the humps "are believed to not be a function of ground reflections between the Mic center and the hard surface below it" (p. 65–66). Chamber test with an omnidirectional source, with/without the ground board: "constructive interference from 140 Hz to 580 Hz … spectral humps … between 670 Hz to 1.2 kHz, 1290 Hz to 1870 Hz, 2030 Hz to 2500 Hz, 2510 Hz to 3110 Hz, and 3120 Hz to 3660 Hz"; levels "increase by at least 6 dB from 150 Hz to 520 Hz, 740 Hz to 1140 Hz, 1340 Hz to 1730 Hz, and 2070 Hz to 2320 Hz with maximum levels of constructive interference of more than 8 dB. Destructive interference greater than 3 dB is shown to occur at 1250 Hz and from 3770 Hz to 6660 Hz" (p. 66–67); "the scalloping that occurs over the mid frequency region is due to edge scattering effects" (p. 68). KEAS airstrip: "No measures were taken to mitigate the effects of constructive and destructive interference resulting from the acoustic reflections on the hard surface" (p. 46).
  - *Outdoor*: background "significant up to 500 Hz" and "obscures the measurement at the BPF" (p. 85–86); RPM fluctuates ~300 RPM and thrust shows "impulse like increases" from gusts (p. 89); Day-2 wind raised LF background and HF broadband (p. 62).
  - *Motor/ESC*: shaft-rate tone (imbalance), ESC switching tone at 18 kHz (≈103×BPF) in all facilities (p. 80, 97).
- **Solutions / mitigations**: Downstream screens (tried; not effective here). Vary FFT averaging time from spin-up and plot tone level vs averaging time with uncertainty εi = 2σ/√Ni to expose recirculation growth (p. 81). Track tones with the measured RPM, integrate a 28–30 Hz band, subtract a 3 s moving average to get tone-level standard deviation (p. 90). Phase-averaging (Zawodny & Boyd) to split tonal/broadband and compare facilities (Sec. 5.1.3). Open jet with minimal inflow removes wake reingestion for the small rotor (abstract). Ground-board response measured in the chamber (ΔLcorr) but "sensitive to the mic location and thus the corrections cannot be directly applied" (p. 68). Windscreens outdoors. Recommendation: for comparison with predictions "an anechoic chamber facility would be recommended when compared to an outdoor facility"; "crude actuator disk models like that of Glegg et al. for a rotor operating in a confined volume may be beneficial" for onset estimation (p. 144). - **Interpretation guidance**: Tone levels that rise with averaging time, only at out-of-plane observers, indicate wake reingestion (unsteady loading radiates along the axis; in-plane levels stay constant) (p. 89). Method of images: null at f = c/(4h) etc. for a mic at height h over a plate; if measured humps don't match, suspect edge scattering of the finite board (p. 65–68). Directivity troughs/humps that move with RPM in a small open jet signal wall/inlet reflection (p. 131). "it is best to assess the strength of higher order harmonics in the broadband and tonal noise to determine the significance of recirculation/turbulence ingestion on the results" (p. 99).
- **Quotable**: "The BPF and its harmonics are all shown to increase by at least 7 dB with increasing averaging time suggesting that the turbulent wake is being reingested." (p. 82) — "None of the results showed a clear delay like observed by Whiteside et al. [19]. It is likely that an effective screen configuration is rotor and anechoic chamber dependent." (p. 84) — "This displays that there may not be a one-size fits all approach to testing rotors like these at static conditions." (p. 99)

## Cross-paper notes (what the texts jointly say; not new claims)
- Recirculation signature (isolated rotor on a stand): BPF unchanged, harmonics ≥2 rise 15–30 dB (Stephenson, Weitsman), ~5–10 dB per tone / ~5 dBA OASPL (Nardari), ≥7–8 dB (Whelchel); strongest along the rotor axis. Free-flying multirotor in a large chamber: OASPL unchanged, tones broaden/split, broadband +1–2 dB (Ma 2024).
- Onset time: ~4–7.5 s (SHAC, 0.24 m rotor), ~30 s (HKUST, free-flying), none detectable in 30 s (VKI with discharge room; CIRA with ~6 D floor clearance and >12 D to the wall). Screens: effective in SHAC (10 s clean), ineffective at Virginia Tech.
- Reflection/standing-wave artefacts: ±4 dB below ~2.2×BPF from room modes (Nardari); up to 10 dB at BPF between adjacent near-floor mics and 3 dB departure from 1/R in hemi-anechoic (Ma 2022); >20 dB comb dips at c/(4h) for a pole mic (Rasmussen); 6–8 dB constructive / >3 dB destructive humps from a finite ground board (Whelchel); filled-in directivity nulls and level-vs-distance ripples from a rigid floor (Fasulo); >5 dB directivity error at one angle at 160 Hz from wedges 0.49 m away (Zawodny); ISO 3745 failures only near corners below 250 Hz (Rizzi).
