  - A rotor whose diameter equals the open-jet inlet diameter breaks the open-jet facility differently: blade tips hit the shear layer, producing directivity troughs/humps that shift with RPM (p.131).
  - Ground-board reflections are not simple image-source nulls here — measured humps are attributed to edge scattering from the finite board, not the predicted image-source nulls (p.66, 68).
  - A facility-independent contaminant exists regardless of confinement — an ESC switching tone at 18 kHz ≈103×BPF appears "in all three facilities" (p.97).
  - For the larger rotor, the open jet — not the chamber — is the noisiest facility at BPF, the reverse of the small-rotor case (p.98–99).
  - Bottom line stated by the author: "there may not be a one-size fits all approach to testing rotors like these at static conditions" (p.99).
- **Relevance**: High — a 4.5×2.5×2.5m chamber with a wire-tensioned stand and an 11-mic line array is close in scale to a small drone-rig chamber, and the key negative result (Weitsman's downstream-mesh trick did not reproduce in a different, smaller chamber) is a direct caution against porting SHAC-specific mitigation numbers wholesale; the facility-vs-facility dB offsets are specific to Whelchel's three rigs/rotor sizes and should not be read as universal constants.

1. **Stephenson 2019 is mislabeled "JASA-EL" (JASA Express Letters) in `MATRIX-3W.md` and `chamber-literature-synthesis.html`.** It is a Letter in the main JASA journal (145(3):1153–1155, DOI 10.1121/1.5092213) — a different journal with a different identity, wrongly propagated from the matrix into the synthesis.
