SoundVisualizer · arc validation · what the chamber does to the polar

The arc is fine above 4 kHz. Below 2 kHz the room bends it by up to 8 dB.

Twelve "horizontal prop" runs (prop7–18, 1–2 Sept 2026, PWM 1900): prop axis perpendicular to the arc, so every microphone sits in the prop plane and a perfect setup reads a circle. The deviation from that circle, with the microphones' own offsets removed, is the room.

What the room does

Indent at −72°: −2.4 to −5.7 dB, 500–2000 Hz, in all twelve runs. Strongest in the 630 and 800 Hz third-octaves (−4.5 dB). Present with microphone 811-1896 in that spot and, in the four runs with the arc turned 180°, with 810-8901 there. This is the position near the chamber corner.
Bump at +36°/+54°: +3 to +7 dB, 200–250 Hz and 630–1000 Hz. Three different microphones sat at +54° across the runs; the bump did not move.
Mirror pattern in the BPF band (200–250 Hz): −36° reads −7.6 dB and +36° +4.8 dB; −54° −3 dB, +54°/+72° +2 dB. Opposite signs above and below the prop plane, in the band the blade-passage tone sits in. The notch moves with RPM (−9 dB at 1900, −2 dB at 2000), the signature of a reflection whose comb dip sweeps through the tone.
Above 4 kHz the arc is a circle to ±1 dB at every position; 0°, ±18° and ±90° are within ~1 dB in every band.

What it is not

Not the microphones, with one exception. A joint fit over the twelve runs separates each microphone's offset from each position's error (they are separable because the mics at +72°/+54° were swapped after prop10 and the arc was turned 180° for prop11–14). Ten microphones sit within ±1.8 dB in every third-octave. 811-1892 reads 2–6 dB low from 125 Hz to 2.5 kHz: its calibration file (Sens Factor −10.63 dB, the least negative of the set) does not match the capsule, whose raw output is ~1 dB low. It made the room indent look 7 dB when it sat at +54°. Retire or re-measure it.

Not a mislabelled arc. prop11, 12, 13 and 14 were measured with the arc turned around (correlation with the mirror image of the other runs +0.79 to +0.98). Read by label their polars show the indent on the other side; read by physical position they land on the others.

Not one frequency. The error lives in three separate bands, 200–250 Hz, 630–1000 Hz and 3150 Hz, and nowhere else (Fig. 2). Rajmane and Baumann (DAGA 2016) placed steel plates in a qualified chamber and found a reflector shows up once the wavelength is no longer than its size: 35 cm from 1.25 kHz, 10 cm only from 3.15 kHz. Three bands mean three reflector sizes: about 1.5 m (floor, table or wall), about 0.4 m (stand base, arc frame member), about 0.1 m (mic clamp or mount).

Where it points

Signs flip across the prop plane, so the reflector is on one side of it, and the low-side positions (−36°, −54°, −72°) carry the deep notches. The RPM-swept BPF notch at −36°/−54° gives a reflected path about 0.7 m longer than the direct one (half a wavelength at 240 Hz). At the arc radius in use that is the floor, the table top or the stand base below the prop, not a far wall. The −72° indent at 630–1000 Hz (λ ≈ 0.4 m) is a smaller object near the low end of the arc on the corner side.
