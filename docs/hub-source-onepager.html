A one-litre box that answers the 238 Hz question

To decide whether the 8 dB hole in the blade-tone polar is the room or the propeller's own inflow, something that is not a propeller has to stand in the same place and play the same frequencies. This is that thing: one 8 cm driver, a sealed cube, about 250 zł. Everything below is computed from the driver's published parameters and from our own September captures.

01Why a cheap single driver is allowed here

Textbook practice for room measurement is a dodecahedron: twelve drivers on a sphere, radiating equally in all directions. The smallest sold is 25 cm and 4.5 kg. Put that at the hub and it becomes the reflector we are hunting.

We escape the requirement through geometry rather than hardware. With the arc laid flat, all eleven microphones sit at the same height in one plane around the vertical axis. Point the driver straight up, the way the propeller blew, and every microphone is then at the same angle from the source. Whatever directivity the box has becomes a single gain shared by all eleven, and our fit already has a term that swallows exactly that: the run gain. It cancels out of the position map, which is the thing we read.

Figure 1 · Side view. Every microphone lies on the source's equator, so the box's pattern is one common gain. It stops being free when the arc goes back upright; the method for that day is in the note.

02The box

External 124 mm cube in 12 mm MDF or birch ply, internal 100 mm cube, one litre. Cutout 75 mm from the datasheet. Butt joints, glue and seal everything, no port, about 10 g of polyester loosely packed, a 6 mm cable hole in the bottom sealed with silicone. Chamfer the four vertical edges 45° × 10 mm: a cube has four-fold symmetry, not rotational symmetry, and the chamfers push its horizontal cross-section closer to a circle.

Try it without an amplifier first. We need about 62 dB at one metre. A laptop output into 8 Ω gives roughly 79 dB. If that measures cleanly the amplifier money stays in your pocket. The catch is not loudness but damping: a headphone output's high source impedance raises Qtc and lengthens the ring in figure 4.

04What it will do at the three tones

In one litre sealed the alignment lands on Butterworth, Fc 181 Hz and Qtc 0.71, which is the flattest and shortest-ringing behaviour a sealed box has. The three blade-passage frequencies we must reproduce sit just above the knee, essentially unattenuated.

Figure 2 · Response of the driver in a one-litre sealed box. At 216, 238 and 258 Hz it is down 1.7, 1.2 and 0.9 dB. The 125 Hz third-octave is down 7 dB, which still leaves 25 dB of margin.

05Loudness is not the problem

Figure 3 · Output on a few watts, limited by cone excursion below 300 Hz, against the requirement. The propeller itself only produced 25 to 62 dB per third-octave at the microphones, and a ten-second sweep adds roughly 50 dB of processing gain on top of everything here.

06The one real constraint: it must stop ringing

We are hunting two echoes: an extra path of 0.7 m at −72°, arriving 2.0 ms after the direct sound, and 1.5 m at +36° and +54°, arriving at 4.4 ms. The source's own decay has to be out of the way by then.

Figure 4 · Decay of the box's own resonance at 181 Hz, with the two echo arrivals marked. It is 20 dB down at 2.9 ms and 30 dB down at 4.3 ms.

Read this honestly: the ring and the far echo overlap. At 4.4 ms the box is only just 30 dB down, so the 1.5 m reflection must be picked from a band-limited impulse response above about 400 Hz, where the box does not ring at all. That is normal practice and it is why the sweep runs to several kHz rather than stopping at 1 kHz. A ported box would be worse here, which is the whole reason for sealing it.

07Order of work

  1. Write the sweep tooling. There is no generator, no playback path and no deconvolution script in the repository today. Until that exists the speaker is an ornament. This part is mine.
  2. Build the cube. An evening. Measure the driver flange when it arrives; Visaton publishes the cutout but not the frame.
  3. Qualify the box before trusting it. One sweep to confirm the decay of figure 4 in the real room. Then rotate the box 45° about the vertical axis and sweep again: what moves with the box is the box, what stays is the room. Ten minutes, and without it nothing that follows is interpretable.
  4. Record the geometry that is still missing: hub height, room size, foam, what stands near the arc ends. Cheapest measurement available and still not done.
  5. Run the session. Motor off, stand in place because its inflow distortion is one of the two hypotheses. Sweep first, then tones at 216, 238 and 258 Hz.
  6. Decide. If the 8 dB pattern returns from the loudspeaker it is the room, and the echo delays name the surfaces. If it does not, it is the propeller's inflow and no amount of foam will help.
When the arc goes back upright the microphones spread over different angles and the cancellation of §01 stops working. Papadakis and Stavroulakis record with one ordinary speaker in twelve to twenty-six orientations and sum the impulse responses, matching a dodecahedron's reverberation time to within 0.08 s (Applied Sciences 8:1703, 2018, held in papers/arc-validation/). Their stated limitation is a two-way speaker's tweeter, which a single full-range driver does not have.
