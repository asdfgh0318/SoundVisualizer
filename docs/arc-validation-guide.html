Why the polar is not a circle

With the prop axis horizontal and perpendicular to the arc, all eleven microphones sit in the prop plane at the same angle to the axis. An axisymmetric source must then read the same at every elevation: the polar should be a circle. It is not. This guide separates the three reasons, one microphone, the arc having been turned around for four runs, and the room, and shows each one on the polar, per third-octave, on the actual data.

02Effect A — one microphone reads low: serial 811-1892

A joint fit over the twelve coherent runs (prop7–18) separates what belongs to each microphone from what belongs to each physical position. Ten microphones come out within ±1.8 dB in every third-octave after their UMIK-2 calibration. One does not.

811-1892 reads 2–6 dB low from 125 Hz to 2.5 kHz and about 1 dB low above. In raw dBFS it is only ~1 dB below its neighbours; the calibration file makes it worse, because its Sens Factor (−10.63 dB) is the least negative of the set (others −11.2 to −13.7 dB), which tells the software this is the most sensitive capsule. It is not. Either the file belongs to another unit or the capsule has drifted since the factory measured it. The extra deficit below 500 Hz is real capsule roll-off (it is in the raw data too).

It sat at +72° until prop10 and at +54° from prop11 (swapped with 810-8901 at the same time as the arc was flipped). Wherever it sat, that spot carried an extra −3 dB. Toggle the correction below and watch the +54° dot in prop18 move back toward the circle; the remaining error at that spot is the room (section 4).

03Effect B — four runs were made with the arc turned 180°

The metadata records each microphone's arc label (+72°, +54° …). If the arc is turned around, the label +e microphone is physically at −e, and the polar drawn by label is a mirror image of the truth. The lab notes did not record which runs those were, so the data were asked: each run's pattern (mic offsets removed) was correlated with the consensus pattern and with its mirror.

Flipped: prop11, prop12, prop13, prop14 (2 Sept 13:18–14:37). Correlation with the mirror +0.79 to +0.98, with the normal orientation −0.19 to −0.01. prop7–10 and prop15–18 are unflipped (+0.63 to +0.95). The 31 Aug runs and prop1–6 correlate with neither orientation (|r| < 0.5): the rig or room was different then, so they are excluded from the fits.

04Effect C — the room: errors fixed to a physical position

After removing run gain and mic offsets, what is left is a pattern tied to the physical position on the arc. Since the source is axisymmetric, this pattern is entirely error: reflections from the floor, the stand, the arc frame, the microphone mounts, or the chamber walls. The heat map is that error per position and third-octave; the polar draws one third-octave at a time.

Three bands, three sizes. The error sits in 200–250 Hz (λ ≈ 1.5 m), 630–1000 Hz (λ ≈ 0.4 m) and 3150 Hz (λ ≈ 0.11 m), and is flat to ±1 dB above 4 kHz. Rajmane and Baumann (DAGA 2016) showed a reflector in an anechoic chamber becomes visible once the wavelength is no longer than its size, so the three bands point at three reflector scales: a floor, table or wall; a stand base or frame member; a clamp or mount. Signs flip between the negative-angle and positive-angle halves of the arc, which is what a reflector on one side of the propeller does. Which room surface that is depends on how the arc stands in the room, and that is not recorded.
The −72° indent. −2.4 to −5.7 dB in 500–2000 Hz in all twelve coherent runs, with 811-1896 there and, during the flip, with 810-8901 there. The +54° bump of +3 to +6 dB is likewise there with three different microphones. Both stay at the physical spot when the arc is turned. That is the definition of a room effect, and it is the one worth chasing.

06Findings and next steps

Microphone

811-1892 is 2–6 dB low below 2 kHz after calibration. Retire it or re-measure it against a good mic side by side; its calibration file is the main suspect. Nothing else on the arc is outside ±2 dB.

Bookkeeping

prop11–14 were measured with the arc turned 180°. Rename them, and record the arc orientation (and the rig geometry: arc radius, prop height above floor, distances to floor, walls, ceiling, table) in docs/mic_arc.md before the next campaign. None of that is written anywhere today.

Room

A stable −4 to −6 dB indent at physical −72° and a +3 to +6 dB bump at +54°, both in 500–2000 Hz, both independent of which microphone is there. Wavelength 0.35–0.55 m: a stand-base or frame-sized reflector on the low side of the arc, near the chamber corner.

The test that names the reflector

Replace the prop with a small loudspeaker on the stand. Play sine bursts (a few cycles at 800 Hz and at 250 Hz, 10 ms apart is enough) and record the arc. At each microphone the reflected wavefront arrives later than the direct one; the delay times 343 m/s is the extra path, and it differs from mic to mic in a way only one surface can satisfy. Rajmane and Baumann matched such lags to geometry within 0.1 m. From the RPM-swept BPF notch at −36°/−54° the extra path at the low mics is already estimated at about 0.7 m. The same speaker run also settles whether any of the error is aerodynamic: with no flow, whatever remains is geometry.

Then treat or move that one surface and re-run scripts/arc_error_map.py. Foam fixes the high band only; if the reflector is a large surface at 200–250 Hz, distance is the only cure.

07Method and provenance

Data: laptop backup of the Pi (../SoundVisualizer-data, 2 Sept 2026 19:29), keys 2004__6in__unset__dp1-baseline-horizontal-*, PWM 1900 step, last capture in each key. Spectra: Welch, 4096-point Hann, 50 % overlap (the server's own compute_fft), UMIK-2 calibration applied exactly as the app does (curve subtracted, Sens Factor added). Levels: third-octave band sums 125 Hz–8 kHz plus the app's default 100 Hz–10 kHz polar band and 500–2000 Hz. Model: L(run, position, mic, band) = g(run) + P(position, band) + M(mic, band), least squares over prop7–18 with prop11–14 mirrored, zero-mean constraints on P and M. Flip classification: correlation of each run's mic-corrected pattern with the reference and its mirror in 500–2000 Hz. Script: scripts/arc_error_map.py; numbers: docs/analysis/*.csv; literature behind the size rule: papers/small-chamber/.
